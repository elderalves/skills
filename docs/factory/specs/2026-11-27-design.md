# Design: the factory designs in Figma, and the owner chooses

Settled in a grilling session on 2026-11-27 (Q1–Q33), starting from a ChatGPT discussion of a "Design Factory" (no figure in it was measured). Terms are in [the glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) under Design (Factory Figma file, Design brief, Variation, Design hypothesis, Exploration, Selection, Refinement, Design review, Design approval, Design handoff, Calibration set). The hard-to-reverse decisions are [ADR 0010](../adr/0010-the-owner-always-selects-the-design.md) (the owner always selects) and [ADR 0011](../adr/0011-designs-live-in-a-factory-figma-file.md) (designs live in a Factory Figma file).

Design is a new kind of work in the same factory: same Linear Factory tracker as the record (ADR 0001), same Trigger owner, sweep, Account pins and per-repo Figma MCP servers. Runs never wait for the owner. Each human step is a status the owner sets, and that change triggers the next run.

## The loop

1. The owner files a Native ticket, or moves an existing Factory ticket, to **Ready for Design**. In v1 nothing else starts design work: Triage does not detect it and Discovery does not propose it.
2. **Exploring** (`alves-factory-design-explore`): write the Design brief and its Design hypotheses, build the Variations in parallel, run one design Council round on each, and post the Selection comment. The ticket then moves to **Awaiting Selection**.
3. The owner gives their **Selection** as a free-text comment ("B, with C's side panel"), then sets the status:
   - **Refining** to refine the selected design.
   - **Ready for Design** to explore again, with the comment saying what to change.
   - **Canceled** to reject all the Variations.
4. **Refining** (`alves-factory-design-refine`): copy or combine the selected design, run Refinement rounds, and add every state and breakpoint. Then write the Design handoff and move the ticket to **Awaiting Design Approval**.
5. The owner approves with one of two statuses:
   - **Ready**: planning starts from the Design handoff.
   - **Design Done**: stop at the design. The handoff stays complete, so planning can start from it at any later time.

## Exploration

- **Brief:** one agent writes the Design brief: the user's goal, requirements, design system constraints, and the states and breakpoints the final design must cover. It also writes three Design hypotheses specific to this ticket. The owner may ask for a different number of Variations on the ticket. Variations that differ only in color, type or spacing do not count as different hypotheses. The brief and the Exploration run happen with no human step in between.
- **Variations:** independent agents run in parallel, one per hypothesis. They all use the same model and get the same brief and design system. None of them sees another's work. Each writes to its own section of the ticket's page in the Factory Figma file. They design only the main screen on desktop, and each agent checks its own screenshot before finishing.
- **One Council round each, no revision:** the scores rank the Variations and feed the Selection comment, and no Variation is revised before the owner chooses.
- **Selection comment:** one factory comment containing, for each Variation:
  - a screenshot uploaded to Linear
  - its Design hypothesis
  - its Council scores and top findings
  - its strengths and trade-offs

  It ends with the Council's recommendation and its reasons, and a link to the ticket's Figma page. The Council recommends but never selects (ADR 0010).

## Design review and gate (`alves-factory-design-review`)

- **Seats:** three, each on a model that can read images, spread over at least 2 distinct models (a design Model floor of 2). Copilot CLI is excluded, because Figma rejects its OAuth.
- **Input:** the rendered screenshot(s) and the Design brief, never the designing agent's own rationale.
- **Rubric (provisional, tuned by the Calibration set):** each criterion is scored 0–10, and every score cites a visible element of the screenshot.

  | Seat | Criteria (weight) |
  |---|---|
  | Product designer | Usability 15, Visual hierarchy 15, Information architecture & flow 10 |
  | Art director | Composition (including spacing and alignment) 15, Typography 10, Visual identity 10 |
  | Design system engineer | Design system consistency 10, Accessibility 10, Figma structure 5 |

- **Veto:** each review lists critical defects separately from its score. Any critical usability or accessibility defect fails the design, whatever its score.
- **Gate:** the weighted score, the veto and the Refinement stop rule are a tested script (ADR 0004), not agent judgment.

## Refinement

- **Works on a copy:** the selected Variation is copied into a "Refined" section, and the original Variations stay untouched. A combination is built as one new frame from the chosen parts before the first round.
- **Coverage:** Refinement adds every state and breakpoint the brief lists (empty, loading, error, mobile, long content, and so on). Design approval requires all of them.
- **Stop rule (pilot):** at most 4 rounds. Refinement stops earlier when either:
  - the weighted score has improved by less than 0.3 in each of the last two rounds, or
  - no critical defect or major finding is left.

  It then hands the design to the owner with each round's score. During the pilot there is no pass mark. After calibration, the pass mark is the Council's score on the designs the owner rated excellent.
- **A score never approves:** clearing the bar ends Refinement. Only the owner gives Design approval.

## Design handoff

One structured factory comment on the Factory ticket, containing:

1. Links to every approved frame (file and node ID), one per screen and state.
2. The Design brief, the owner's Selection reasons (structured from their comment) and their Design approval note.
3. A mapping from each frame to library components, with every element that has no counterpart in code marked **new component needed**.
4. The tokens and variables used, and every value that is not a token.
5. Behavior Figma doesn't show: interactions, transitions, validation and data rules.
6. Open concerns from the last Design review round.
7. The Figma file version at approval.

**Frozen:** planning compares the file's current version with the one in the handoff. If the approved frames have changed, it stops with a Blocker, and the owner either re-approves or reverts. A Plan built from a handoff cites the approved frames as its spec. Code Connect is not part of v1.

## Figma

- **Factory Figma file** per project (ADR 0011): owned by that repo's Figma account and using the team's published library, with one page per Factory ticket. On that page, each Variation has its own section, and Refinement adds a "Refined" section. Approved frames go into the team's file only when the owner says so. For the pilot, the factory file and the team's file are the same, because the owner owns the product.
- **Accounts:** through the repo's local-scope `figma-<account>` MCP server, so each design is made as the right account. Claude, Codex and OpenCode work.

## Calibration

- **Calibration set:** three briefs taken from real features of the pilot repo. For each brief the owner adds one or two reference designs they rate highly, and scores every result blind on the rubric.
- **Through the real pipeline:** each brief is a Native Factory ticket labeled `calibration`, run by the real `alves-factory-design-explore`. The arm is a setting on the ticket, and the owner's scores go in a comment. Calibration needs only Exploration.
- **First run, two arms on the same model:**
  - **Direct:** Figma MCP with `figma-use` and `figma-generate-design`.
  - **Browser first:** HTML in a throwaway worktree of the pilot repo, off its default branch, as a temporary route using the app's real components and styles. It is served locally, captured into Figma with `send_code_to_figma`, then deleted, and never committed or pushed.

  3 briefs × 2 arms × 3 Variations = 18 designs. They are compared on the owner's scores, the Council's scores, Figma editability (auto layout, real components), cost and time.
- **Third-party skills** (Uizze, UI/UX Pro Max, Impeccable, Anthropic Frontend Design and others) are tried one at a time on the winning arm, in later Calibration runs. A skill joins only as a copy the owner has read, vendored into `skills/` and pinned to a commit, with its frontend-output instructions adapted to Figma. Paid MCPs stay out of v1.
- **Cost:** every design run records its time and token use on the Factory ticket. There is no budget during the pilot. One is set after the first Calibration run.

## Operations

- **Sweep:** Exploring and Refining are running statuses with a Stale limit, so a dead design run is restarted once. Awaiting Selection and Awaiting Design Approval wait on the owner and are never swept.
- **Taste:** for now, only Selection reasons are recorded, as structured comments on the Factory ticket. Taste files are distilled after about 10 design tickets.

## Pilot and build order

Pilot repo: **gerador-de-escala** (personal account, Vite + React + Radix). It has no Figma library.

0. Build the pilot's Figma library from its code with `figma-generate-library` (tokens from `styles.css`, components from the Radix-based UI). The owner reviews it.
1. `alves-factory-design-review`, its gate script and the rubric.
2. `alves-factory-design-explore`, the new Linear statuses and the Selection comment.
3. First Calibration run: 3 briefs × 2 arms. Choose the arm and tune the rubric weights.
4. `alves-factory-design-refine` and the Design handoff.
5. Connect the handoff to planning, with the frozen-design check.
6. Sweep coverage of Exploring and Refining.
7. Third-party skills, one at a time, through Calibration.
